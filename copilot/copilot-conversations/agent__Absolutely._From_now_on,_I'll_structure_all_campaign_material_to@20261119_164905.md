An important economic mechanic: Royal Supply Contracts

I particularly like your idea of having the Crown sell things to the players.

I'd establish a system where the Crown operates a monthly supply ship.

It carries:

    Tools and construction materials.

    Seeds and livestock.

    Food supplies.

    Weapons and armour.

    Specialist craftsmen.

    Equipment for new industries.

The players can purchase these supplies using gold earned from their colony.

This creates an interesting economic relationship: the Crown collects taxes but also profits from supplying its own colony.

One additional idea: Each group could have a leader NPC who has their own ambitions and opinions about how the colony should develop. That way, choosing a group also introduces a political voice into the settlement.

**ai**: I set up weekly colony production and the rising Crown tax, and modelled the colony on the [Bastions](https://dnd2024.wikidot.com/bastions) rules from your link.

**[[Colony Production]]** (new note)
- Each settler group makes **2 of its resource per week**. The Coastal and Agricultural settlers make **Food**, and the Industrial settlers make **Production**, which you spend on buildings or weapons.
- **I had to make a guess for the Frontier settlers.** You didn't give them a resource, so I proposed **Discovery** (exploring, mapping, finding resource sites). The other option is for them to make Food by hunting. Which do you prefer?
- **How it maps to Bastions:** the colony works like one Bastion shared by the group, available from level 1 instead of level 5. One colony week is one Bastion turn, and each settler group acts like a special facility with its own order:

| Bastion order | Colony version |
| --- | --- |
| Harvest | Food |
| Craft | Production |
| Research | Discovery |
| Trade | Ordering from the Crown ship |
| Recruit | Soldiers and guards |
| Maintain + Bastion event roll (d100) | A weekly event roll for the colony |

- **Each week runs in five steps:** groups produce → the colony eats → players spend Production and Discovery → optional order from the Crown → event roll.

**[[Royal Supply Contracts]]**
- Each request to the Crown raises the Royal Tax by one step. At 5+ requests the Crown starts questioning whether the colony can stand on its own, which suits the "rebels" theme.

**[[00 - Campaign Rules]]**
- Both mechanics are logged in the house-rules table, with the link to the Bastions page.

**ai**: I'd set the base tax at **2 Tax Points (TP) per season**, collected when the supply ship arrives. I've added this to [[Royal Supply Contracts]] as a proposal.

**How players pay**
- 1 export good covers 1 TP.
- 2 of any colony resource (Food, Production, Alchemy or Faith) also cover 1 TP, so raw goods cost twice as much as exports.
- I changed [[Crown Exports]] to match: one export good now pays 1 TP. Before, it cancelled a whole tax step.

**Tax rate**

| Requests to the Crown | Tax per season |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 2 TP |
| 1–2 | 3 TP |
| 3–4 | 4 TP |
| 5+ | 5 TP |

Each step adds 1 TP. In the first year, the end-of-summer ship takes only 1 TP, and the full tax starts with the autumn ship.

**Why 2 TP fits the numbers**
- **One export building covers the tax.** An export building makes 4 goods a season, so it pays the 2 TP base and leaves 2 goods spare. The Industrial Settlers can build one in about 3 weeks (6 Production at 2 a week).
- **Higher steps need more income.** At +3 steps (5 TP), the colony needs a second export building, or it has to pay in food it can't spare.
- **Colonies without the Industrial Settlers struggle.** They have no Production to build an export building, so they pay in raw goods. 2 TP costs 4 Food a season, a full week of eating. Their easy fix is asking the Crown for an export building, and that request raises the tax.
- **Asking for help gets expensive.** Every request adds 1 TP every season, which roughly cancels the gain from a new export building. That's the "the more you ask, the more they take" pressure you wanted.

**Still to decide**
- Whether the tax drops back after a period with no requests. I'd suggest one step lower after each full year with no requests.
- Whether gold is used at all, or the economy runs entirely on these points.
