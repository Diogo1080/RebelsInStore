**ai**: A good first expedition is a scouting mission for water. Your expedition notes already make "secure a reliable water source" the colony's top priority, and a water hunt naturally takes the party out into the wild lands.

## First Expedition: *The Water Survey*

**Hook:** On the second day of the landing, [[Expedition Commander|Halloway]] gives the party their first order: *"The barrels won't last. Find fresh water within a day's march, chart the way there, and be back in three days."* [[Navigator|Ashby]] offers to come along "to draw the maps". He's the only one who asks to go.

**Goal:** Find a water source the colony can rely on, get back alive and report what's out there.

### The Route (3 days, 4 sites)

**1. The Brackish River Mouth**
- The obvious answer, but the water is salty near the coast. The party has to follow the river upstream.
- **Encounter:** Giant Crabs in the tidal mud, which makes a good easy first fight.
- **Clue:** old wooden stakes in the riverbank. Someone was here before.

**2. The Fog Marsh**
- A shortcut through the marsh, or a long detour around it, costs half a day.
- **Hazards:** sinking mud (Athletics/Survival checks) and Stirges at dusk.
- **Choice:** the marsh is full of edible roots and fish, which would make it a good second food source for the [[The Agricultural Settlers|farmers]] or [[The Coastal Settlers|fishers]].

**3. The Overgrown Camp**
- The remains of a failed camp: collapsed huts, rusted tools and a half-burned journal.
- The journal mentions "the spring under the stones" and "the ones who watch from the trees". Leave it open whether it was an earlier colony or a local people.
- **Encounter:** Wolves, or a Brown Bear that has made the camp its den. The party can fight it, scare it off or sneak past.

**4. The Stone Spring**
- A clean spring that never runs dry, coming out of a ring of carved standing stones. It's perfect for the colony, but a 6–8 hour walk from the landing site.
- **The twist:** the stones are clearly sacred to someone, with fresh offerings left at their base. Is it safe to take the water?
- **The secret:** a **silver vein** runs through the rock behind the spring.

### Why It Works for This Campaign
- **Ashby's first test:** he "accidentally" spots the silver and writes it down. The party sees him do it, or doesn't, depending on their Perception. That's an early clue for [[Navigator|the spy plot]], and the first decision about what the Crown gets to know.
- **The first real colony decision when they return:**
  - Move the camp inland to the spring (safer water, but further from the ship and the sea)
  - Build a supply route between the ship and the spring (costs workforce)
  - Keep looking (costs days of rations)
- **Report to Halloway:** do they mention the silver and the sacred stones? The answer affects the Crown's attitude later through [[Royal Supply Contracts]].
- **Sets up the wild lands:** the journal and the offerings show the land isn't empty.

### Tuning
- Built for **level 1–2**. Use 2024 Monster Manual creatures: Giant Crab, Stirge, Wolf, and a Brown Bear as the "boss".
- Track rations and Exhaustion lightly so the trip feels like survival.
- **Total:** 2–3 fights, 1 social or moral decision and 1 political secret, which fits a 1–2 session adventure.

**ai**: I've added both bear options to [[The Water Survey]] and switched it to milestone levelling.

**Distracting the bear (the peaceful way)**
- The party lures it out of the camp with **food bait**, a **false scent trail**, or **noise elsewhere** (for example, rolling rocks downhill or casting *Minor Illusion*).
- It takes one DC 12 check, and the bear wanders off for an hour.
- On a failure, the bear comes out irritated and the party gets one last chance to back away before it attacks.

**Befriending the bear (the hard way)**
- It takes **three rounds of Animal Handling**: approach slowly (DC 13), offer food by hand (DC 14), then stay close while it eats (DC 15).
- Three successes win the bear's trust. Two failures mean it attacks with Advantage on its first hit.
- *Speak with Animals* or a druid or ranger character gives Advantage on the checks.
- **Hidden reason it's grumpy:** a rusted arrowhead from the old camp is stuck in its shoulder. Removing it counts as one automatic success.
- **Reward:** the trail to the spring becomes safer, the bear may help the party once in a later adventure, and it could shape how the locals first see the colony.

**Milestone levelling**
- The adventure now ends with a milestone: the party reaches **level 2** when they report back to Halloway. Fighting, distracting or befriending the bear all count the same.
- I also logged milestone levelling as a house rule in [[00 - Campaign Rules]], replacing XP from defeated creatures.
- I added an open thread to the note to track whether the bear was befriended.

**ai**: I've added an **"Ashby the guide"** callout to [[The Water Survey]], just below the safety-net tip.

**How it works:**
- Ashby watches for danger on the trail (Passive Perception 14).
- He speaks up only **after** the players have had a chance to notice something themselves, or when they're about to walk into danger.
- He gives advice and lets the players decide. He always says it modestly: *"I'm only a mapmaker, but..."*

**What he says at each site:**
1. **The stakes:** he points out the fish bones and the bubbles, and suggests poking the mud. That nudges the party to set off the crab ambush themselves.
2. **The marsh:** he tells them to walk where the reeds grow thick, which gives Advantage on the sinking-mud checks. He also suggests keeping a fire going against the stirges.
3. **The camp:** he recognises the bear's den and lists the options: go around, feed it, or (he wouldn't recommend it) fight it.
4. **The spring:** *"Someone tends this place. I'd touch nothing until we know who."*

**The spy angle:** a hidden DM comment explains that his advice is always genuinely good. He wants a grateful party that talks freely. I also added an open thread to track what the players confide in him, since all of it ends up in his report to the Crown.
