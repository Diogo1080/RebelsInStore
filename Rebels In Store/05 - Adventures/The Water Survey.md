# The Water Survey

The party's first expedition into the wild lands. They need to find fresh water the colony can rely on, chart the way there and get back within three days.

## Overview

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Given by** | [[Expedition Commander\|Commander Halloway]] |
| **Companion** | [[Navigator\|Navigator Ashby]] |
| **Party** | 3 players, level 1 (level 2 at the end) |
| **Length** | 1–2 sessions, 3 in-game days |
| **Encounters** | 2–3 fights, 1 moral choice, 1 political secret |
| **Creatures** | 2024 Monster Manual: Giant Crab, Stirge, Wolf, Brown Bear |

## The Hook
Day 2 of the landing (see [[The Royal Expedition#Landing and Encampment]]). The water barrels on [[The Royal Fortune]] are going down fast.

> [!quote] Read aloud: Halloway's orders
> Halloway unrolls a blank sheet of chart paper on a supply crate and taps its empty centre. *"This is everything we know about this land. Nothing. The barrels won't last the month. Find me fresh water within a day's march, chart the way there, and be back in three days. If you're not back by then, I'll assume the worst."*

Before the party leaves, [[Navigator|Ashby]] volunteers to come along "to draw the maps". He's the only one who asks to go.

## Encounter Balance (3 players, level 1)
Using the 2024 DMG XP budget for three level-1 characters: **Low 150 XP · Moderate 225 XP · High 300 XP**.

| Encounter | Creatures | XP | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Site 1: Ambush at the Stakes | 2 Giant Crabs | 50 | Low (good warm-up, even if surprised) |
| Site 2: Fog Marsh | 3 Stirges | 75 | Low |
| Site 3: Overgrown Camp | 1 Wounded Brown Bear | ~150 | Low to Moderate |
| Site 3 (alternative) | 2 Wolves | 100 | Low |

Characters at level 1 have very few Hit Points, so all fights are kept at **Low or Moderate**. The party should finish the trip tired and short on resources, but alive.

> [!tip] Safety net: Ashby
> Ashby avoids fighting ("I'm a mapmaker, not a soldier"). But if a character drops to 0 Hit Points, he rushes over and **stabilises them**, then gives them a sip of brandy that restores 1 Hit Point. This keeps a bad roll from killing a character, and makes the party like and trust him, which is exactly what a spy wants.

> [!tip] Ashby the guide
> Ashby watches for danger on the trail (Passive Perception 14) and gives the party friendly advice. He's useful but never takes over: **he advises, the players decide.** Every good tip earns a little more of their trust.
>
> **How to run it:**
> - He speaks up **after** the party has had a chance to notice something themselves, or when they're about to walk into danger.
> - His advice should give the players a plan, not solve the problem for them.
> - He always phrases it modestly: *"I'm only a mapmaker, but..."*
>
> | Site | What Ashby notices | What he advises |
> | --- | --- | --- |
> | 1. The stakes | Fish bones and bubbles in the mud | *"Fish don't die in neat little piles. Something eats here. I'd poke that mud before I stepped in it."* This hints that the party can set off the trap first |
> | 2. The marsh | Which ground is firm, and the stirges' nests in the reeds | *"Walk where the reeds grow thick: the roots hold the mud together. And keep a fire going tonight, those little things in the reeds hate smoke."* Gives Advantage on the sinking-mud checks |
> | 3. The camp | Fresh claw marks and droppings around the big hut | *"That's a bear's larder, not a ruin. We can go around, feed it, or... well. I wouldn't fight it."* Lists the party's options |
> | 4. The spring | The fresh offerings | *"Someone tends this place. I'd touch nothing until we know who."* |
>
> %% DM: Every piece of advice is genuinely good. Ashby wants the party alive and grateful, because grateful people talk freely. Keep a note of what the party confides in him on the trail: it ends up in his next report to the Crown. %%

## Survival on the Trail
- **Rations:** the party gets 3 days of rations from [[Quartermaster|Brannock]]. Every extra day out costs rations they don't have.
- **Navigation:** at the start of each day, one character makes a **DC 12 Wisdom (Survival)** check. On a failure, the party loses half a day.
- **Exhaustion:** a night without shelter or water, or a forced march past 8 hours, means a **DC 10 Constitution** save. On a failure, the character gains 1 level of [Exhaustion](https://dnd2024.wikidot.com/general:conditions).

## 1. The Brackish River Mouth
A wide, slow river runs into the sea a mile north of the landing site.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> The river spreads into a maze of tidal mudflats and reeds. Gulls scream overhead. The water looks clear, but it tastes of salt.

- **The water:** a **DC 10 Wisdom (Survival)** or **Intelligence (Nature)** check shows the tide pushes salt water far upstream. The party has to follow the river inland.
- **Clue:** a line of old, sharpened wooden stakes stands rotting in the mud at the water's edge, easy to spot from the bank. Up close, a **DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation)** check shows they were cut with metal tools. Someone was here before.

### Ambush at the Stakes
**2 Giant Crabs** (CR 1/8) have buried themselves in the mud around the stakes, where fish get trapped at low tide. The stakes draw the party straight to them.

> [!quote] Read aloud: when the party approaches the stakes
> The mud around the stakes is churned and pitted, littered with fish bones and broken shells. Small bubbles rise from it with a wet pop, every few seconds.

- **Spotting them:** roll **one Dexterity (Stealth) check for the crabs (+4)**. Compare it with each character's **Passive Perception**. Anyone who actively searches the mud before stepping in can make a **Wisdom (Perception)** check instead.
- **Clues for sharp players:** the fish bones, the bubbles, and the gulls that avoid landing near the stakes. If a player says the mud looks wrong, give them Advantage on the check.
- **The ambush:** the crabs burst out when someone steps within 5 feet of a stake. Every character who didn't beat the crabs' Stealth roll is **surprised**, which in the 2024 rules means **Disadvantage on their Initiative roll**.
- **Turning the tables:** if the party spots the crabs before anyone steps in, they can spring the trap on their own terms. Some ways they might:
  - Throw a fish or a ration into the mud, or poke it with a pole or spear from 10 feet away.
  - Ready attacks or spells around the stakes before the crabs come out.
  - Throw a rock or fire an arrow at a bubbling patch of mud.

  The crabs burst out to grab whatever disturbed them and find the party waiting. Now **the crabs are surprised** and roll Initiative with Disadvantage. The party also chooses where to stand, so they can keep out of the mud and make the crabs come to them on dry ground.
- **Some spotted, some didn't:** if only some characters spotted the crabs, they can warn the others or set the trap. If nobody steps in, nobody in the party is surprised.
- **Terrain:** the mud around the stakes is **Difficult Terrain** for the party, but not for the crabs.
- **Tactics:** each crab grabs the nearest character with a claw and drags them towards the water. If a crab drops below half its Hit Points, it buries itself again and retreats.

It's an easy first fight, but the surprise and the mud should make the players nervous about the wild lands from the very first hour.

## 2. The Fog Marsh
Half a day upstream, the river spreads into a wide marsh.

**Choice:**
- **Go through it:** faster, but dangerous.
- **Go around it:** safe, but costs **half a day**.

If they cross:
- **Sinking mud:** each character makes a **DC 12 Strength (Athletics)** or **Wisdom (Survival)** check. On a failure, they're stuck and lose an hour, or take 1d6 Bludgeoning damage pulling themselves out.
- **Encounter at dusk:** **3 Stirges** (CR 1/8) come out of the reeds. A stirge's blood drain is dangerous at level 1, so have them fly off once they've drained a few Hit Points rather than staying attached to one character.
- **Discovery:** the marsh is full of edible roots, eels and waterfowl. Reporting this gives the colony a second food source, worth an extra Food for [[The Coastal Settlers]] or [[The Agricultural Settlers]] (see [[Colony Production]]).

## 3. The Overgrown Camp
On high ground beyond the marsh, the party finds the remains of a camp.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> Vines have swallowed a ring of collapsed huts. Rusted tools lie in the grass where they were dropped. In the centre is a blackened fire pit, and something large has been sleeping in the biggest hut.

- **Encounter:** a **Brown Bear** (CR 1) has made the largest hut its den. It's the toughest fight of the trip, so give the party options:
  - **Fight it.** The bear is **wounded** (see its stat block), which makes this a fair fight for three level-1 characters. It flees when it drops to 5 Hit Points or fewer.
  - **Scare it off:** fire, loud noise, or a **DC 13 Wisdom (Animal Handling)** check.
  - **Sneak past:** a group **DC 12 Dexterity (Stealth)** check.
  - **Distract it (the peaceful way):** see below.
  - **Befriend it (the hard way):** see below.
  - *Alternative:* use **2 Wolves** (CR 1/4) for a pack fight instead.

### Distracting the Bear
The bear is hungry, not hostile. The party can lure it out of the camp without a fight. Each idea needs **one successful check** (DC 12), and the bear wanders off for an hour:
- **Food bait:** leave a ration, fish from the river or honeycomb from a nearby tree (getting the honey costs 1d4 bee stings, 1 Piercing damage each). A **Wisdom (Survival)** check places the bait far enough away. *Cost:* 1 day of rations, unless they use fish or honey.
- **False trail:** a **Wisdom (Survival)** check drags a fresh carcass or fish scent away from the huts.
- **Noise elsewhere:** a **Dexterity (Sleight of Hand)** or **Strength (Athletics)** check rolls rocks or breaks branches down the slope, or a spell such as *Minor Illusion* draws it off.

On a failure, the bear isn't fooled. It comes out of the hut irritated and the party gets one last chance to back off before it attacks.

### Befriending the Bear
Harder, slower and riskier, but the reward lasts. The party has to win the bear's trust over **three rounds of approach**, each time making a **Wisdom (Animal Handling)** check (other characters can Help):

| Round | What the party does | DC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Approach slowly, no weapons drawn | 13 |
| 2 | Offer food by hand (costs 1 day of rations) | 14 |
| 3 | Stay close while it eats, touch it or tend to it | 15 |

- **Three successes:** the bear accepts them.
- **Two failures:** the bear attacks. Roll initiative, and it has Advantage on its first attack.
- *Speak with Animals* or a Druid/Ranger background gives Advantage on every check.
- **Hidden reason it's grumpy:** a **DC 13 Wisdom (Medicine or Perception)** check spots a rusted arrowhead in its shoulder, from the old camp. Removing it (DC 12 Medicine) counts as one automatic success.

**Reward:** the bear stays around the old camp and the spring. It won't follow the party, but:
- The trail between the landing and the spring becomes safer: wolves and scavengers avoid its territory.
- It may turn up in a later adventure to help the party in a fight (once, DM's choice).
- The people who leave offerings at the stones may have seen it. A colony that befriends the forest's animals makes a very different first impression.
- **The journal:** under the floor of one hut is a half-burned journal (**DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation)**). Readable fragments:

> [!note] Journal fragments
> *"...water fouled again. Tomas swears the spring under the stones is sweet, but the others won't go near it..."*
>
> *"...the ones who watch from the trees left something on the path again. Feathers and bone. A warning or a gift?..."*
>
> *"...we leave at first light. God help whoever comes next."*

%% DM: Decide later who built this camp: an earlier failed colony, smugglers, or another nation's expedition. "The ones who watch from the trees" are a local people, introduced properly in a later adventure. %%

## 4. The Stone Spring
About 6–8 hours' walk from the landing site.

> [!quote] Read aloud
> Cold, clear water bubbles up into a pool, surrounded by a ring of tall standing stones covered in carvings worn smooth by time. At the foot of the largest stone, someone has left fresh flowers, a bundle of feathers and a carved wooden bowl. They can't be more than a day old.

- **The water:** clean, cold and plentiful. A **DC 10 Intelligence (Nature)** check confirms it never runs dry.
- **The stones:** a **DC 14 Intelligence (Religion or History)** check shows they are old, maintained and clearly sacred. The offerings are recent: the people who left them are nearby and still use the site.
- **The secret:** behind the largest stone, the rock face holds a **vein of silver** (**DC 14 Wisdom (Perception)**, or automatic with proficiency in mason's or smith's tools).

> [!danger]- DM ONLY: Ashby and the silver
> Ashby spots the silver whether the party does or not. Have each character make a **Wisdom (Perception)** check against his **Deception +7** (Advantage from *False Face*). On a success, they see him quietly sketching the rock face in his journal and covering the page when he's noticed.
>
> If confronted, he laughs it off: *"A cartographer marks everything, my friend. Rocks included."*
>
> Whatever the party reports, **the Crown learns about the silver** through Ashby's next dispatch, unless the party finds a way to stop him. See [[Navigator#Clues for the Party]].

## Back at the Landing: The Report
The party reports to [[Expedition Commander|Halloway]]. Two questions matter:

**1. What do they tell him?**
- **The silver:** if Halloway learns about it, he's obliged to report it to the Crown. If the party hides it and Ashby reports it anyway, the Crown learns the party keeps secrets. That raises Crown distrust (see [[Royal Supply Contracts]]).
- **The sacred stones:** Halloway's instinct is practical: *"Water is water."* The party may need to argue for caution.

**2. What does the colony do about water?** The party recommends, Halloway decides.

| Option | Benefit | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Move the camp inland** to the spring | Safe, permanent water | Far from the ship and the sea; [[The Coastal Settlers]] unhappy; trespasses on a sacred site |
| **Build a supply route** from ship to spring | Keeps the coastal camp | Ties up settlers every week hauling water |
| **Keep searching** | Might find a better site | Costs more days of rations |

## Rewards
- **Milestone:** the party reaches **level 2** when they report back to Halloway, however they handled the encounters. Fighting, distracting or befriending the bear all count the same.
- A **map of the region**, the colony's first chart of the wild lands.
- **The marsh** as a new food source, if discovered.
- **The journal**, which opens the next adventure: who were the previous settlers, and who are "the ones who watch from the trees"?
