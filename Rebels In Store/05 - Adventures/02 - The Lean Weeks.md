# The Lean Weeks

The colony's stores are shrinking faster than its fields and nets can fill them. The party has four days to find food sources the colony can rely on, and they can't search everywhere.

## Overview

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Given by** | [[Expedition Commander - Edric\|Commander Halloway]], with [[Quartermaster - Isolde\|Brannock]]'s ledger |
| **Companion** | The leader of one of the colony's food groups (see below) |
| **Party** | 3 players, level 2 (level 3 at the end) |
| **When** | Late spring of year 1, a few weeks after [[01 - The Water Survey]] |
| **Length** | 1–2 sessions, 4 in-game days |
| **Structure** | 3 leads, time for only **2** of them |
| **Creatures** | 2024 Monster Manual: Giant Octopus, Wolf, Swarm of Insects |

## The Hook
Brannock has done the sums. Every colony start loses Food in its first spring (see [[Colony Production#Food: Balance Check|the balance check]]), and the relief ship is still weeks away.

> [!quote] Read aloud: in the commander's tent
> Brannock lays her ledger open on the table and runs a finger down a column that only goes one way. Halloway doesn't look at it; he's already seen it. *"We eat more than we catch and more than we grow. At this rate the stores are gone before the harvest. I need food we can count on: not a lucky catch, a place we can go back to. You have four days."*

### Gearing Up
As before, the party collects their gear and **4 days of rations** from Brannock (see [[01 - The Water Survey#Gearing Up]]).

### The Companion
Halloway wants someone who knows food to judge what they find, but **the party chooses** who comes with them, and **the party chooses** which leads to follow. He gives no orders on either.

The party can take **one** companion:
- **[[Coastal Leader - Marta|Marta]]**, if the colony has [[The Coastal Settlers]]. She favours the sea and grumbles on any inland trail.
- **[[Agricultural Leader - Aldous|Fenwick]]**, if it has [[The Agricultural Settlers]]. He favours open land and wants to clear it.
- **A soldier**, always available.

The companion never fights unless cornered, but gives Advantage on one check per site that fits their trade (fishing, farming, hunting).

| Companion | Oyster Reef | Elk Meadows | Wild Orchard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Marta** | Best: reads the lights at sea, knows the tides | Gets hurt at the pit trap (see the lead) | No special effect |
| **Fenwick** | Disadvantage on Stealth in the lagoon | Recognises the wild grain automatically; backs hunting past the post | Best: his farming Advantage fits the orchard, the hives and the old terraces |
| **Soldier** | No special effect | Best: Advantage on Perception | No special effect |

The choice of companion and route is the party's first big decision. Each pairing changes what they find and what it costs.

> [!note] Ashby stays behind
> [[Navigator - Corwin|Ashby]] stays at camp this time, "copying the charts". He asks the party for a full account when they return. %% DM: the party's absence gives him a free hand in camp; see Back at Camp. %%

## The Three Leads
Each lead takes **1 to 1½ days** to reach and search. With 4 days, the party can follow **two** of them and get back. Following all three means a fifth day without rations (see [[01 - The Water Survey#Survival on the Trail|Survival on the Trail]]).

> [!important] The pull onward
> Whichever lead the party visits **last**, use its **Pull onward** box: just as their rations run out, they find something bigger one more day away. Going after it means a day with **no rations**: each character makes the **DC 10 Constitution** save for Exhaustion at the end of it (see Survival on the Trail), and Halloway notices they're late. The reward is worth it, and the players should feel torn.

| Lead | Where | Found by | Food type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. The Oyster Reef** | South along the coast | Marta's or a fisher's tip, or seen from the [[The Royal Fortune\|ship]] | Shellfish (Coastal) |
| **2. The Elk Meadows** | Inland, past the spring | The bear's tracks or Ashby's map from [[01 - The Water Survey]] | Hunting |
| **3. The Wild Orchard** | Up the river, beyond the marsh | Fruit stones in the old camp's middens, or the forest people's paths | Fruit and honey |

> [!tip] If the ship stays
> If the colony kept [[The Royal Fortune]], the party can sail south to the Oyster Reef in **half the time** (see [[The Royal Fortune#Option A Exploring by Sea|Exploring by Sea]]). That buys time for the third lead.

### Trail Events
Each lead has its own journey and night camp below. For extra colour on a long stretch, roll a d6 once per half-day:

| d6 | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Rain.** Everything gets soaked. Fires need a **DC 12 Wisdom (Survival)** check, or the night counts as without shelter |
| 2 | **Lost bearings.** The trail splits. Navigation check (DC 12) or lose 2 hours |
| 3 | **Snake in the path.** A Constrictor Snake or poisonous snake on the trail. Easy to avoid if spotted (**DC 12 Wisdom (Perception)**) |
| 4 | **Forager's luck.** Mushrooms, berries or eggs: a **DC 12 Wisdom (Survival)** check saves half a day of rations |
| 5 | **Watched.** Someone is following. A **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception)** check catches a glimpse of a painted face before it's gone |
| 6 | **The companion's story.** The companion opens up about their past or their hopes (see their NPC note). A good moment to win them over |

## 1. The Oyster Reef
A half-day south of the landing, a rocky point shelters a lagoon full of oyster and mussel beds.

> [!tip] Marta at the reef
> If [[Coastal Leader - Marta|Marta]] is the companion, this is her ground: she's spent more of her life on rocks and boats than on land. On this lead:
> - **She knows the tide.** She calls the party out of the pools before the lagoon floods, so nobody has to make the Strength (Athletics) check to avoid being swept towards the cave.
> - **Advantage on Wisdom (Survival) and Intelligence (Nature)** checks about the sea: the shellfish, the octopus, blocking the cave, the tidal channel.
> - **She reads the lights at sea** and can answer the smugglers' signal (see Night Camp and the pull onward). In a parley, she gives Advantage on every Charisma check.

> [!note] The soldier at sea
> If **the soldier** is the companion, they're no sailor, but they're steady and do as they're told. No bonus and no penalty on this lead, except that they **fight alongside the party** if it comes to blows with the smugglers (see *Fight at the Fire*).

> [!warning] Fenwick at sea
> If [[Agricultural Leader - Aldous|Fenwick]] is the companion here, he's a strong, capable man, but a farmer, not a fisher. He climbs the collapsed path without trouble. In the water, though, he's clumsy: he wades heavily and splashes. While the party is in the lagoon, they have **Disadvantage on Dexterity (Stealth)** checks.

### The Way There
The coast path runs along low cliffs above the beach.
- **The collapsed path:** halfway, a stretch of cliff has fallen into the sea. Climbing across takes a **DC 12 Strength (Athletics)** check (on a failure, 1d6 Bludgeoning damage from a slide onto the rocks), or the party goes down to the beach and loses 2 hours waiting for the tide.
- **The seal colony:** on the rocks below, a colony of seals. Easy meat, if anyone wants it (**+1 Food**), but [[Coastal Leader - Marta|Marta]] refuses: *"Kill the seals and the fish go with them."* Whether she's right or superstitious is up to the players.
- **Waiting for the tide:** they arrive at high tide and have to wait **3 hours** for the beds to show. Use it: a fishing contest with the companion, scouting the cave from above, or setting up the net to block it.

### At the Reef
> [!quote] Read aloud
> At low tide the lagoon drains to a field of black rock crusted with shells. Pools glitter between them. At the far end, a sea cave swallows the water with a slow, heavy breath.

- **The beds:** a **DC 10 Intelligence (Nature)** check confirms the shellfish are safe to eat, and plentiful.
- **The danger:** a **Giant Octopus** (CR 1) lives in the sea cave and hunts in the pools at low tide. Anyone wading near the cave risks being grabbed.
- **The tide:** the beds can only be worked at low tide. Every few hours the lagoon floods; anyone still out in the pools must make a **DC 12 Strength (Athletics)** check or be swept towards the cave.

### Dealing with the Octopus *(Moderate)*
- **Fight it:** at low tide it's half out of the water and easier to hit. It retreats into the cave when badly hurt.
- **Block the cave:** a net of rope or driftwood across the mouth (**DC 13 Wisdom (Survival)** or tools) keeps it inside while the colonists work.
- **Feed it:** leaving it fish each day keeps it in the cave. A cheap peace, but someone has to keep doing it.

**What the colony gets:** the **Oyster Beds** as a work site. Each week a group (or idle hands) can work them for **+1 Food** in spring, summer and autumn (nothing in winter). With [[The Coastal Settlers]], working them is automatic and needs no order.

### Night Camp: Lights at Sea
The party camps on the point above the lagoon.

> [!important] Only on the second lead
> The lights at sea, and everything that follows from them (Marta at dawn, the smugglers' cargo), only happen if the Oyster Reef is the **second** lead the party visits. If it's their **first** lead, the night is quiet: only the glowing sea. The smugglers haven't arrived yet.

- **The sea glows.** The water in the lagoon sparkles green when disturbed. A good moment to let the players breathe.
- **A light offshore.** Around midnight, whoever is on watch sees a **lantern** far out at sea, blinking in a pattern, then gone. The party can tell it's a pattern, but not what it means:
  - A **DC 17 Intelligence (History)** check recognises it as a **smuggler's signal**, though not the exact message.
  - A character proficient in a **smugglers' code** (such as Thieves' Cant, or a smuggler or sailor background, DM's call) reads it fully: *"cargo aboard, looking for a quiet beach"*.
  - Otherwise, only Marta can read it.
- **Fenwick's reaction** (if he's the companion): he squints at it, shrugs and rolls over. *"Trick of the light. Moon on the water. Go back to sleep."* He forgets about it by morning, and the lights stay a mystery until someone who knows the sea sees them.
- **Marta's reaction** (if she's the companion): she goes quiet, then says she saw nothing. A **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight)** check shows she knows exactly what it was. See her secret in [[Coastal Leader - Marta]].
- **If the party presses her:** she won't admit anything. She changes the subject (the weather, the gulls, a story about her sons), gets louder the harder they push, and finally walks off to "check the nets".
  - Pushing her costs the party some of **Marta's favour**: for the rest of the trip she stops helping (no Advantage from the companion), and back at camp she's cool with them.
  - She won't share her smuggling contacts until they earn her trust back, for example by keeping quiet about the lights in front of [[Navigator - Corwin|Ashby]] or Halloway.
- **If the party lets it go:** she says nothing that night. **The next morning**, while the others pack up, she takes them aside:

  > [!quote] Read aloud: Marta at dawn
  > *"That light last night. Three long, two short. It means 'cargo aboard, looking for a quiet beach'. My father taught me that before he taught me to gut a fish. Somebody out there is trading on this coast, and it isn't the Crown."* She looks at each of you in turn. *"I'd keep that between us, if I were you. Especially from the mapmaker."*

  - She explains the signal and that she can answer it, but not yet her family's full past (see her secret in [[Coastal Leader - Marta]]).
  - The party has earned her respect: she'll help them freely for the rest of the trip, and she's the one to ask if the colony ever wants to trade without the Crown.

> [!question]- Pull onward: the smugglers' cargo
> The morning after the [[#Night Camp Lights at Sea|lights at sea]], the party spots a **thin line of smoke** rising from the coast a day further south, where no smoke should be. If Marta read the signal, she knows what it is: *"That's the quiet beach. They've landed."*
> - **Getting there:** a long day over dunes and tidal flats. Halfway, a channel cuts across the beach and has to be crossed at low tide (**DC 12 Strength (Athletics)**, or wait 3 hours).
> - **The cove:** a narrow inlet hidden behind a rock spur. A two-masted ship rides at anchor offshore, flying no flag. On the beach, a dying fire and fresh boat tracks lead to a **sea cave** where the cargo is stacked.
> - **The guards *(Low)*:** **2 Bandits** (CR 1/8) watch the cave and play dice. They aren't expecting anyone. A **DC 13 Dexterity (Stealth)** check (as a group) gets the party close unseen. Fenwick's splashing doesn't apply here; this is dry sand.
> - **The cargo:** barrels of **salted pork and ship's biscuit (+4 Food)**, casks of brandy, and bolts of fine cloth. The brandy and cloth are worth **1 export good** if sold to the Crown, or traded through Marta.
>
> **What the party does:**
>
> If [[Coastal Leader - Marta|Marta]] is with them, she pushes hard for a parley: *"Don't rob them and don't fight them. Talk to them. People like that are worth more as friends than as enemies."*
>
> | Choice | Effect |
> | --- | --- |
> | **Steal the cargo** | +4 Food and the brandy and cloth. The smugglers will want to know who robbed them, and **a raid on the colony will follow** (see below) |
> | **Make contact** | Only if Marta is with them, or someone can answer the signal. The smugglers' captain comes ashore to parley (see below). **Either way, the captain now knows the colony exists** |
> | **Watch and leave** | Nothing taken. The party knows where the smugglers land, and can report it, or keep it secret |
>
> **Covering their tracks:** after a theft, the party can hide where the cargo went: brushing out footprints, leaving false trails towards the forest, or dumping the empty barrels at sea. Make a group **DC 13 Wisdom (Survival)** check.
> - **Success:** the smugglers can't tell who robbed them. The raid is **delayed**: it only comes much later, once they've tracked the cargo down (for example, when they see the colony selling brandy, or someone talks).
> - **Failure, or no attempt:** the trail leads straight north to the colony. **The raid will come**, soon (within a season).
>
> **The parley:** the captain rows ashore with four armed crew and sits on a barrel by the fire. They want to know who the party is, how many are in the colony, and what it has worth trading. Run it as a short negotiation: the party needs **3 successes before 2 failures** on **DC 13** checks:
> - **Charisma (Persuasion)** to offer a fair deal, **Wisdom (Insight)** to read what the captain really wants, or **Charisma (Deception)** to bluff about the colony's strength.
> - **Marta**, if she's there, gives **Advantage on every Charisma check** in the parley. She speaks their language, and the captain knows her kind.
> - Threatening them counts as an automatic failure. **Threaten them a second time** and the captain is furious: they kick over the barrel and order the crew to attack (see *Fight at the Fire* below).
> - **Lies:** a failed Deception check means the captain caught the lie. They don't say so: they smile and carry on, and the DM counts it as a failure in secret. Right after any lie, one character can make a **DC 14 Wisdom (Insight)** check to read the captain:
>   - **Success:** they know whether the lie landed or was caught. If it was caught, the party can own up and change course (the failure stands, but the next check isn't affected).
>   - **Failure:** they can't tell, and the captain's smile gives nothing away.
>
> | Result | Effect |
> | --- | --- |
> | **It goes well** | A trade route: once a season, the smugglers swap **Food for colony goods, outside the Crown's tax** (rates to be set). The first step towards rebellion |
> | **It goes poorly** | The captain leaves politely, but now knows where the colony is, how small it is and what it has. **A raid on the colony follows later in the campaign** (DM's timing: a season or two, or when the colony is weakest) |
>
> **Fight at the Fire *(High)*:** the **captain** (use a **Bandit Captain**, CR 2) and **4 crew** (**Bandits**, CR 1/8) fight on the beach by the fire. This is a hard fight for three characters at this level.
> - The crew break and run for the boats if the captain falls.
> - The captain tries to escape by boat when below half Hit Points. Stopping them takes a chase across the sand to the water.
> - **It settles the raid:** if the captain is killed or captured, the smugglers lose their leader and won't come for the colony. **No raid follows.** If the captain escapes, the raid comes, and soon.
> - Marta won't fight her own kind. She hangs back, and is shaken afterwards.
> - **The soldier fights.** If the soldier is the companion, they join the fight on the party's side (use the **Guard** stat block, 2024 Monster Manual). That makes this fight much more even.
> - **If the party is defeated:** the smugglers don't kill them. They take the party's **weapons and magic items** (the [[01 - The Water Survey#The Bear's Hoard|Cloak of Elvenkind]] included) and sail off, leaving them bruised on the beach at 1 Hit Point. **The raid comes the very next week**, while the colony's best fighters are still unarmed. And [[Quartermaster - Isolde|Brannock]] wants to know where her weapons went.
>   - **If [[Agricultural Leader - Aldous|Fenwick]] is the companion,** he doesn't run. He picks up an oar and wades in to protect the party, and when they fall, he's beaten down too. He's left **Incapacitated and badly hurt** and can't walk. Before the party can head home, someone has to heal him: any magical healing, or a **DC 13 Wisdom (Medicine)** check and a day's rest. Until then he must be carried (the return takes **half a day longer**).
>
> - **Why it matters:** someone is trading on this coast behind the Crown's back. If [[Navigator - Corwin|Ashby]] hears of it, so does the Crown. If [[Coastal Leader - Marta|Marta]] hears of it, she already knows exactly who to talk to.

## 2. The Elk Meadows
A day inland, past the spring, the forest opens into long grass meadows where elk graze.

> [!tip] The soldier in the forest
> If **the soldier** is the companion, they're at home here: trained to watch the treeline and the ground. On this lead, the party has **Advantage on Wisdom (Perception)** checks (spotting the pit trap, the wolves, the watchers around the camp).

> [!warning] Marta in the forest
> If [[Coastal Leader - Marta|Marta]] is the companion, she's on unfamiliar ground, and she gets hurt at the **pit trap** (see The Way There):
> - **If nobody spots the trap,** she's the one who falls in.
> - **If they spot it,** she steps around it onto a hidden root and wrenches her knee.
>
> Either way, she can't walk. The party has to deal with it:
>
> | Option | Effect |
> | --- | --- |
> | **Heal her** | Any magical healing (a spell or a potion) gets her back on her feet. Using one of the party's few healing resources this early hurts |
> | **Splint it** | A **DC 13 Wisdom (Medicine)** check (a healer's kit gives Advantage). She can limp, but the party moves slowly: this lead takes **half a day longer** |
> | **Carry her** | Two characters take turns carrying her. The party moves at half speed (**half a day longer**), and each carrier makes the **DC 10 Constitution** save for Exhaustion at the end of the day |
> | **Leave her at the spring** | She waits at the Stone Spring until they come back for her. She's safe, but furious, and the party loses **Marta's favour** (as if they had pressed her about the lights) |
>
> **If the bear was only driven off** in [[01 - The Water Survey]] (scared or distracted, not killed or befriended), it still roams between the old camp and the spring. Leaving Marta there is fatal: when the party returns, they find the bear's tracks and **Marta is dead**.
> - [[The Coastal Settlers]] lose their leader. They blame the party, and the colony holds a funeral before anything else.
> - Her smuggling contacts die with her.
> - Give the players a hint before they choose: fresh claw marks on a tree near the spring, or a **DC 12 Wisdom (Survival)** check that finds the bear's tracks.

### The Way There
The trail passes the [[01 - The Water Survey#4. The Stone Spring|Stone Spring]] again.
- **The offerings have changed.** How depends on what the party did on their first visit:
  - **Touched nothing:** a new carved bowl has been left, facing the colony's direction. A gift, or a welcome.
  - **Took or damaged something:** the offerings are gone, and a line of stones has been laid across the path. A warning.
  - **Took the silver or dug at the rock:** the same, and a cut rope hangs from the largest stone.
- **The pit trap:** in the forest beyond, a covered pit lined with stakes sits on an elk trail (**DC 13 Wisdom (Perception)** to spot; on a failure, the first character falls in for 2d6 Piercing damage). It's freshly dug and well made. Someone hunts here.

### At the Meadows
> [!quote] Read aloud
> The meadow is alive: elk move through the grass in a slow brown tide, birds rise from it in clouds. At its edge, a tall post stands driven into the ground, carved with antlers and hung with old bones.

- **The hunt:** a **DC 13 Wisdom (Survival)** check to stalk the herd, then attacks or traps. A good hunt brings back enough meat for **+2 Food** (one-off).
- **The wolves *(Low)*:** **3 Wolves** (CR 1/4) follow the herd. If the party hunts, the wolves come for the kill that night.
- **The marker post:** a **DC 12 Intelligence (History or Religion)** check shows it marks a boundary. These are someone else's hunting grounds: the people who watch from the trees.

### The Choice
| What the party does | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Hunt here anyway** | +2 Food now, and the meadows become a hunting ground: foraging from here gets Advantage. The forest people's attitude drops one step (see [[Colony Production#Either Way\|Signs from the trees]]) |
| **Respect the boundary** | No meat. If they leave something at the post (food, a tool), the attitude rises one step |
| **Ask permission** | Leave a gift and wait a night. The next morning, a strip of red cloth is tied to the post: permission for one hunt (+2 Food, no attitude change). Hunting again later without asking still drops the attitude |

%% DM: this is the closest the party comes to the forest people before their proper introduction. They are watching the whole time. Nobody shows themselves. %%

### Night Camp: The Visitors
The party camps at the meadow's edge.
- **Wolves howl** around the herd all night. If the party hunted, the wolves come for the kill (see above).
- **The companion** (if Fenwick) talks about his land grant and how much good land this is. If the party mentions the marker post, he shrugs: *"A post isn't a deed."*
- **In the morning**, small bare footprints circle the camp. Nothing is taken. But the party's food bundle has been **retied with a different knot**, and a handful of **dried berries** has been tucked inside. Someone came close enough to touch their things and chose to leave a gift.

> [!question]- Pull onward: the golden valley
> At sunset the elk move off east, and following them with the eye, the party sees a valley beyond the next ridge, gold from end to end. A **DC 12 Intelligence (Nature)** check (automatic for Fenwick) recognises it: **wild grain**, a whole valley of it.
> - **If they go:** it's barley and a wild wheat, ripening early.
> - **Getting there:** the ridge is steep forest with no trail (**DC 13 Wisdom (Survival)** to find a way up, or lose half a day). From the top they can see smoke rising far to the north: the first sign of where the forest people live. A sack of seed heads gives the colony **seed stock**: [[The Agricultural Settlers]] get **+1 Food every summer**, from next year on (or, without them, it's worth +2 Food as a one-off).
> - **Why it matters:** the valley lies past the marker post, deep in the forest people's land, and it has been harvested before: stalks are cut in neat rows. Taking from it without asking drops their attitude one step. [[Agricultural Leader - Aldous|Fenwick]] will want to move the whole colony here.

## 3. The Wild Orchard
Up the river beyond the marsh, a sheltered valley holds an old, overgrown orchard: apple and pear trees gone wild, and hives in the hollow trunks.

### The Way There
The party follows the river past the [[01 - The Water Survey#2. The Fog Marsh|Fog Marsh]] and the [[01 - The Water Survey#3. The Overgrown Camp|old camp]].
- **The old camp again:** if the party killed or befriended the bear, it's quiet. If they only scared it off, the bear is back, and it remembers them.
- **The ford:** beyond the camp, the river must be crossed. It's fast and chest-deep (**DC 12 Strength (Athletics)**, or swept 50 feet downstream for 1d6 Bludgeoning damage). Roping the party together gives Advantage. Building a raft takes 2 hours.
- **The fish weir:** above the ford, a woven fence of sticks across a side channel traps fish. It's old but repaired with fresh reeds, and full of fish. Taking them is **+1 Food**, but it's someone else's trap: the forest people's attitude drops one step. Leaving something in exchange doesn't change it.

### In the Orchard
> [!quote] Read aloud
> The trees grow in rows, too straight to be wild. Fruit hangs heavy and unripe on the branches. The air hums; the trunks are hollow, and every hollow is full of bees.

- **Who planted it:** a **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** check finds old pruning cuts and a rotted ladder. Someone tended this long before the old camp. It might be older than both colonies.
- **The bees *(Moderate)*:** taking honey disturbs **2 Swarms of Insects** (CR 1/2). Smoke (**DC 12 Wisdom (Survival)**) calms them and avoids the fight.
- **Too early:** the fruit won't ripen until **autumn**. This is an investment, not a rescue.

**What the colony gets:**
- **Honey:** if they take it, **+1 Food** now.
- **The Orchard** as a work site: **+2 Food a week in autumn**, from this year on.
- If [[The Religious Order]] is in the colony, the hives are a ready start for their **Apiary and Chandlery** (see [[Crown Exports]]): it costs **2 Production less**.

### Night Camp: The Honey Thief
The party camps under the orchard trees. The air smells of blossom and wax.
- **At midnight the humming stops.** Something large is moving between the hollow trunks.
  - **If they befriended the bear** in [[01 - The Water Survey]], it's the bear, raiding a hive. It recognises them and lets them sit near it while it eats. A moment of peace, and the companion will tell everyone back at camp.
  - **Otherwise**, it's a **Black Bear**, younger and shyer. It flees from fire or noise; it only fights if cornered.
- **Glowing fungus** rings the oldest tree, the only one with no hive. Under it, a **DC 14 Wisdom (Perception)** check finds a small carved stone half-buried in the roots, like the carvings at the spring.

> [!question]- Pull onward: the keeper's house
> At the head of the valley, half a day's climb further, the party glimpses a **stone roof** among the trees, and a path from the orchard leads straight to it, still worn.
> - **If they go:** it's a small stone house, older than the orchard.
> - **Getting there:** the path climbs through terraces, old stone walls holding up fields gone to forest. Someone farmed this whole valley once. Under the floor is a **sealed cellar** with clay jars of dried fruit, honey and nuts, still good (**+3 Food**), and carvings on the walls like those on the [[01 - The Water Survey#4. The Stone Spring|standing stones]].
> - **Why it matters:** whoever planted the orchard also raised the stones. The carvings show people tending trees, and a ship arriving from the sea. Someone came before, and they didn't leave a camp: they left a home. This answers part of "who planted the orchard?" and raises a bigger question.

## Back at Camp
The party reports to Halloway and Brannock. Brannock writes every find into the ledger.

- **Each lead found** is worth one line in the ledger and one argument among the leaders: Marta wants the reef, Fenwick wants to clear the meadows for fields, and [[Religious Leader - Agathe|Mother Agathe]] (if present) wants the hives.
- **What the party says about the marker post** matters. Halloway's instinct is practical again: *"Meat is meat."* Fenwick agrees with him. The party can argue for caution.

> [!danger]- DM ONLY: Ashby in camp
> While the party was away, Ashby went through Brannock's stores "looking for ink". A **DC 13 Wisdom (Insight)** check on Brannock notices she's uneasy. If asked, she says the medical locker was opened and closed again, but nothing is missing. He now knows about the [[Expedition Supplies#Medical Reserve|hidden potions]].

## Rewards
- **Milestone:** the party reaches **level 3** when they report back.
- **Work sites** for each lead they secured: the Oyster Beds, the Elk Meadows hunting ground, or the Orchard.
- **One-off Food:** from the hunt (+2) or the honey (+1).
- **The pull onward**, if they followed it: the smugglers' cargo (+4 Food, or a trade contact), the grain valley's seed stock, or the keeper's cellar (+3 Food), plus the story it opens.
- **The forest people's attitude**, changed by what they did at the marker post.
