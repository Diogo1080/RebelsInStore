# Royal Supply Contracts

The Crown runs a **supply ship once per season** (every 4 weeks) to the colony. Players pay for its cargo with gold, colony resources or export goods.

## The First Shipment
The Crown knows a brand-new colony has nothing to give yet. The **first supply ship** arrives at the end of spring. It is a **relief shipment**, not a tax collector:
- **No tax is collected** and **no exports are expected**.
- It does **not** count as a request to the Crown.

The colony chooses one of two packages:

| Package             | Cargo                | Cargo (no [[The Industrial Settlers\|Industrial Settlers]]) | Cost                                   |
| ------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| **Standard relief** | 4 Food, 2 Production | 2 Food, 4 Production                                         | Free                                   |
| **Double relief**   | 8 Food, 4 Production | 4 Food, 8 Production                                         | The colony owes the Crown **1 Favour** |

A colony without the Industrial Settlers only has the base 1 Production a week, so the Crown sends building materials instead of some of the food.

### Choosing the Next Group
On the first ship, the colony also tells the Crown which [[Settler Groups|settler group]] it needs most. That group **arrives free on the next ship** (end of summer), and it does not count as a request.
- It must be one of the **settler groups** not chosen at the start (Coastal, Agricultural or Industrial). Specialist teams can only come later, as paid groups.
- The new group starts producing the week it lands. Its people eat from then on (a group of 20 is **+1 Food upkeep**).
- It needs its own Shelters before winter (see [[Colony Production#Buildings (proposed)|Buildings]]).

### Later Groups
After the free group, more settlers have to be paid for. The colony asks for a group on one supply ship and it arrives on the next one.

| Group | Cost | Also |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Settler group (20 people) | **3 TP** (750 gp) | Counts as a request to the Crown |
| Specialist team (10 people) | **4 TP** (1,000 gp) | Costs more because of their specialist equipment. Counts as a request to the Crown |

**Paying in full:** the colony can pay extra, **+1 TP** for a settler group (4 TP) or **+2 TP** for a specialist team (6 TP), so the group does **not** count as a request. The Crown is happy to sell settlers to a colony that pays well.

Tax Points are paid the same way as the tax: export goods, resources or gold (see [[Royal Supply Contracts#Paying the Tax (proposed)|Paying the Tax]]).

### Crown Favours
A Favour is a debt the Crown can **call in at any time**, usually at the worst possible moment. Examples:
- Send soldiers or settlers on a Crown errand for a season
- Ship an extra export cargo for nothing
- Host and protect a royal envoy, inspector or noble
- Carry out a delicate or unpleasant task for the Crown

Refusing to honour a Favour counts as defiance. The Royal Tax rises 1 step, and the Crown's distrust grows.

From the summer ship onwards, the normal rules below apply.

## Typical Cargo
- Tools and construction materials
- Seeds and livestock
- Food supplies
- Weapons and armour
- Specialist craftsmen, including groups not chosen at the start (see [[Settler Groups]])
- Equipment for new industries

## Escalating Royal Tax
The more the colony asks of the Crown, the more the Crown takes. The tax is collected **each season, when the supply ship arrives**.

### Paying the Tax *(proposed)*
The tax is counted in **Tax Points (TP)**. Each TP can be paid with:

| Payment | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| 1 export good ([[Crown Exports]]) | 1 TP |
| 2 of any colony resource (Food, Production, Alchemy, Faith) | 1 TP |
| **250 gp** | 1 TP |

Export goods are the efficient way to pay. Raw resources work, but cost twice as much. Gold is the expensive way out: the base tax of 2 TP costs 500 gp, the same as building a Cramped basic facility in the [Bastion rules](https://dnd2024.wikidot.com/bastions). At low levels it's for emergencies only, such as paying with treasure from adventures.

### Tax Rate *(proposed)*

| Requests to the Crown | Royal Tax per season |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | **Base: 2 TP** |
| 1–2 | 3 TP (+1 step) |
| 3–4 | 4 TP (+2 steps) |
| 5+ | 5 TP (+3 steps); the Crown begins to question the colony's self-sufficiency |

- **1 step = +1 TP.**
- **Year 1 grace:** the end-of-summer ship collects only **1 TP**. The full base applies from the end-of-autumn ship.

### Why 2 TP
- **One export building** makes 4 goods a season. It pays the base tax and leaves 2 goods to sell or save.
- **At +3 steps**, the colony needs a second export building, or has to give up Food it can't spare.
- **Colonies without the Industrial Settlers** only have the base 1 Production a week. With Standard relief (4 Production) they reach a 6-Production export building in about 2 weeks of summer; Double relief (8) covers one straight away, at the price of a Favour. They can also make up the gap by [[Colony Production#Foraging and Gathering|gathering materials]], at the cost of foraged Food. Until then they pay in raw resources: 4 Food a season is a full week of upkeep, so it hurts. Asking the Crown for an export building is the obvious fix, and it raises the tax.

### Self-Sufficiency Review
After a **full year (16 weeks) with no requests** to the Crown:
- **All request steps are cleared.** The tax drops back to the base.
- **The base rises by 1 TP** (2 → 3 → 4…). The Crown now considers the colony self-sufficient and expects more from it.

A colony that stands on its own escapes the penalties for asking, but each year of independence makes the Crown's share permanently larger. This feeds the colony's resentment towards the Crown.

**Royal Debt:** if [[The Royal Fortune]] is dismantled, the colony owes the Crown **4 TP** (1,000 gp). The Royal Tax is 1 step higher until the debt is paid. Paying it off does **not** remove the Crown's distrust; that stays for good.

## Trading the Surplus *(proposed)*
Each supply ship (every 4 weeks), the colony settles up in this order:
1. **Pay the tax** (see above). The tax always comes first.
2. **Trade the surplus:** anything left over can be traded with the ship for **Food, Production, Faith or Alchemy**.

| The colony gives | It gets |
| --- | --- |
| 1 export good | 2 of any resource |
| 2 of any other resource | 1 of any resource |
| 125 gp | 1 of any resource |

Each ship carries only so much. Per ship, the colony can get at most:

| Resource | Limit per ship |
| --- | --- |
| Food | 4 |
| Production | 4 |
| Faith | 1 |
| Alchemy | 1 |

- Trading surplus is a fair exchange, **not a request**, so it doesn't raise the Royal Tax.
- What the Crown sends arrives with the ship, ready for the next week.
- Asking for Food or Production **without** paying in goods or gold (on credit, or as an emergency) is still a request.

The rates match the tax: 1 export good is worth 1 TP, and so are 2 resources. The Crown always keeps its cut, so swapping one resource for another loses half.

To keep things simple, the only other thing on offer is:

### Rare Magic Items
The colony can order a **rare magic item** for **4 TP each** (4 export goods, 8 resources or 1,000 gp).
- The players name the item they want when the ship arrives; it comes on the **next ship**.
- The DM can refuse items that would break the campaign, or offer something similar instead.
- Like other trades, a paid order is not a request.

## Design Intent
The Crown collects taxes **and** profits from supplying its own colony. Asking for help is always an option, but it makes the Crown's grip tighter, which suits a campaign called *Rebels in Store*.
